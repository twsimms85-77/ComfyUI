# Best AI Tax Prep for a Solo CPA — Research & Recommendation

*Research compiled August 2026, as a follow-up to the SurePrep analysis. Goal: identify AI tools a solo practitioner can adopt and offer as a client-facing service.*

The market splits into three categories that solve different parts of the workflow. "AI tax prep" as a service offering really means stacking one or two of these.

## Category 1 — AI prep engines (the AI drafts the return)

These ingest client documents, extract the data, and push a drafted return into your existing tax software for your review and signature.

| Tool | Pricing | Tax software supported | Best fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Juno** (juno.tax) | ~$45/return, dropping to low $30s at volume; flat per return | Drake, Lacerte, ProConnect, CCH Axcess | Solo and small-to-mid firms |
| **Filed** | ~$5–20/return, complexity-based | Broadest engine + practice-management coverage | Small-to-mid firms; cost less predictable |
| **Black Ore Tax Autopilot** | Unpublished, demo/enterprise pricing ($60M a16z-backed) | ATX, CCH, Drake, Intuit, UltraTax (1040 live) | High-volume enterprise shops — skip as a solo |

Juno automates ~90% of data entry with a human in the loop and integrates with TaxDome. Its flat pricing means mid-stream document additions don't change the cost; Filed's complexity-based pricing can drift as a return evolves (note: Filed's own comparison content ranks the category, so treat its claims accordingly).

## Category 2 — AI client intake / organizer automation (fixes document chase)

These replace the paper organizer and PBC chase — often the actual bottleneck in a solo practice.

- **StanfordTax** — reads prior-year returns, generates a personalized questionnaire, then sorts, bookmarks, and renames uploaded documents into tax-return order. Connects to CCH Axcess, UltraTax, Lacerte, ProSeries, Drake, and ProConnect. **Free up to 5 returns; Premium ~$18/return.** The free tier makes it a zero-risk pilot.
- **Soraban** — AI-driven admin assistant: customizable questionnaires with automatic SMS/email/voicemail reminders, document retrieval, workflow management. Heavier on client-communication automation.
- **TaxCaddy** (SurePrep/Thomson Reuters) — covered in the prior analysis; capable but priced and built for larger firms, with post-acquisition stagnation complaints.

## Category 3 — AI research/advisory copilots (internal leverage, advisory upsell)

- **TaxGPT** — research, drafting, document analysis, client-response writing; solo practitioners report multi-hour daily time savings. Starter tier for document summarization/chat.
- **CPA Pilot** — $89/user/month ($950/yr annual). Reviewers say it's excessive under ~50 returns/year; better suited to firms selling planning engagements.

## Recommendation for a solo practice

**Stack StanfordTax + Juno.**

1. **Start with StanfordTax now** — free for 5 returns, ~$18/return after. It fixes intake (the part clients actually feel) and its document-sorting output makes any downstream prep faster whether AI or manual.
2. **Pilot Juno on next season's returns** — it's the prep engine actually built for solo/small firms with transparent flat pricing. At ~$30–45/return cost against a typical $400+ prep fee, margin holds even without raising prices. Confirm it supports your tax software before committing (Drake, Lacerte, ProConnect, CCH Axcess today).
3. **Skip** Black Ore (enterprise-only), full SurePrep (priced for mid-size+, stagnating), and CPA Pilot (uneconomical below ~50 returns) for now.

## Packaging it as a service

- **Don't sell "AI prep" per se** — sell what it enables: a "priority/express return" tier (faster turnaround), a fully digital drop-and-done experience (photo-upload intake, no organizer), or expanded capacity to take on new 1040 clients without hiring.
- **Price the experience, not the tool**: e.g., an express-turnaround surcharge or a premium digital tier; the ~$20–60/return combined tool cost is absorbed several times over.
- **Engagement letter language**: disclose technology-assisted preparation with CPA review and signature — you remain the preparer of record; the AI never files or advises unreviewed.
- **Vet before season**: run 3–5 of your own prior-year returns through any pilot and check the AI's output against the filed return — that's your accuracy benchmark, and per-return pricing means the trial costs almost nothing.
