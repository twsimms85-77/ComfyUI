# SurePrep Reviews — Positives, Negatives, and Analysis

*Research compiled August 2026. SurePrep is the Thomson Reuters tax workflow automation suite: 1040SCAN (OCR/data extraction), SPbinder (digital workpapers), and TaxCaddy (client document collection portal). Thomson Reuters acquired SurePrep for $500M, closing January 2023.*

---

## Positives

**Best-in-class document recognition and workpaper process.** Practitioners on TaxProTalk consider SurePrep "best in class" for recognizing tax documents and for its 1040 workpaper workflow — and note it is priced accordingly. Its patented AI auto-verifies data on roughly 65% of standard source documents (W-2s, 1099s, 1098s), which is the strongest OCR accuracy claim in the category.

**Real efficiency gains at scale.** Firms report verification time and cost dropping around 15% with auto-verification. A large NYC firm (Janover LLC) case study reported significantly improved realization using 1040SCANverify. It flags missing source documents and prevents duplicate data export.

**Broad tax software integration.** Exports to essentially every professional package — UltraTax CS, GoSystem, CCH Axcess, Lacerte — so it isn't locked to Thomson Reuters preparers.

**TaxCaddy is generally liked by clients.** Practitioners report most clients take to it. Combined with 1040SCAN it can nearly eliminate paper: clients photograph/upload documents or use bank "smart links," sign engagement letters and 8879s, and pay through the portal.

**Support included.** Unlimited email, chat, phone, and screen-share support at no extra cost. G2 reviewers say new staff learn it quickly.

**Outsourcing option.** SurePrep Outsource covers 1040, 1041, 990, 1065, 1120, and 1120S prep, priced by return complexity — useful for capacity crunch without hiring.

## Negatives

**Weak on complex returns.** The most consistent criticism: it is not good with K-1s, rental properties, Schedule C, and property sales. The AI shines on standardized documents and degrades on anything nonstandard — exactly the work where a preparer's time is most expensive.

**Not worth it for simple returns.** For a couple of W-2s and a 1099-INT/DIV, the scan-verify-export loop costs more time and money than just typing the numbers in.

**Stagnation since the Thomson Reuters acquisition.** A widely-shared LinkedIn post by Logan Graf, CPA (55+ comments) reports "zero innovation since the acquisition," glitchy error-prone imports, clunky navigation, and that the onshore review add-on felt like a waste of money. This post-acquisition sentiment is the loudest recent complaint theme.

**Verification overhead.** Non-auto-verified data still requires a human verification pass. Firms mitigate by assigning it to interns/admin staff, but a solo has no one to hand it to.

**Learning curve and tool sprawl.** It takes real time to learn, and it is one more system on top of your tax software. Practitioners with an already-solid paperless workflow question the marginal benefit.

**TaxCaddy friction for clients.** App reviews cite: the Wells Fargo smart link failing almost daily and needing resets; no batch upload (one document at a time gets tedious); documents/messages accidentally landing in the wrong tax year; small hard-to-read text; account lockouts (3 failed logins = 2-hour lock) and SMS verification snags. Support is business-hours Pacific with ~24-hour ticket turnaround — awkward during crunch.

**Opaque pricing.** No published pricing; everything is quote-based through Thomson Reuters sales, which makes cost comparison hard for small firms. Tiering (1040SCAN Organize / Pro / verify / Trades) adds complexity.

## Analysis

SurePrep's value is a function of firm size and return mix, not software quality in the abstract.

**Where it wins:** mid-size to large firms running hundreds-to-thousands of moderately complex 1040s heavy on standard broker/employer documents, with admin or offshore staff to absorb the verification step. There, the ~15% verification savings, missing-document flagging, and standardized SPbinder workpapers compound into real realization gains, and TaxCaddy solves document chase at scale.

**Where it loses:** the two ends of the spectrum. Simple returns don't repay the per-return cost and process overhead. Complex returns (K-1s, rentals, Schedule C, dispositions) defeat the OCR and still require manual work. A solo or very small firm whose book is concentrated at either end will likely find the quote-based pricing hard to justify — and a solo also personally absorbs the verification step, the learning curve, and every TaxCaddy support call from clients.

**Trajectory risk:** the pre-acquisition product earned its "best in class" reputation; the post-2023 complaint pattern (no innovation, glitchy imports) suggests Thomson Reuters is harvesting rather than investing. Competitors (e.g., StanfordTax, Soraban, and newer AI-native document tools) are targeting exactly the small-firm segment SurePrep prices out.

**Bottom line for a small practice:** if your book is dominated by standard-document 1040s and client document collection is your bottleneck, TaxCaddy alone (or a lighter portal) may capture most of the benefit. Full 1040SCAN + SPbinder only pencils out with enough volume of the "right-shaped" returns and someone to do verification. Demand a per-return cost quote and pilot it on a representative slice of your book for one season before committing.
